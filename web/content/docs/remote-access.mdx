## Pairing

Codync runs on your computer. Pair your iPhone or Android phone by scanning the QR code shown in Codync on that computer. This fork does not require a Codync account. Your phone connects to the computer over one of these routes:

| Route | When it works | Setup |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wi-Fi | Your phone and computer are on the same local network | None |
| Tailscale | Both devices are signed in to the same tailnet | Install Tailscale on each device and sign in to your Tailscale account |
| Cloudflare Tunnel | You want a stable public hostname | Install `cloudflared` on the computer and use a domain managed by your Cloudflare account |
| Your own HTTPS URL | You already run a reverse proxy or tunnel | Set `CODYNC_EXTERNAL_URL` on the computer and forward `/channel` to Codync |

Codync prefers working direct routes. Tailscale and Cloudflare provide the network path; Codync pairing keys still decide which phones can connect.

## Tailscale

Install [Tailscale for macOS](https://tailscale.com/docs/install/mac), [iPhone](https://tailscale.com/docs/install/ios), or [Android](https://tailscale.com/docs/install/android). Sign in on the phone and computer to the same tailnet. Tailscale supports account sign-in through providers such as Apple, Google, GitHub, and Microsoft; see [Tailscale identity providers](https://tailscale.com/docs/integrations/identity).

The Codync host detects its Tailscale address and MagicDNS name and includes them in pairing links. When both devices are on the tailnet, they connect directly without opening an inbound port on your router. Tailscale's [official install guide](https://tailscale.com/docs/install) covers other host platforms.

## Cloudflare Tunnel

Cloudflare Tunnel gives the computer a stable public hostname without opening an inbound port on your router. You need a domain added to Cloudflare, with its nameservers pointed to Cloudflare, and the official [`cloudflared` program](https://developers.cloudflare.com/tunnel/downloads/) installed on the computer.

In Codync, open **Reach from anywhere** for the paired computer, choose **Sign in to Cloudflare**, and finish the browser sign-in. If the browser is on another device, use the sign-in link shown by Codync. Enter a hostname from your Cloudflare domain, such as `codync.example.com`, then choose **Create tunnel**. Codync creates a named, locally managed tunnel and its DNS record in your Cloudflare account, then starts it when the computer is running. The `cloudflared` process stays on the computer; Codync stores its tunnel credentials in the system vault.

This is Cloudflare's locally managed tunnel workflow. Cloudflare currently recommends remotely managed tunnels for most general deployments; those are configured from Cloudflare's dashboard. See the [Cloudflare tunnel setup guide](https://developers.cloudflare.com/tunnel/features/locally-managed-tunnels/create-local-tunnel/) and [tunnel permissions](https://developers.cloudflare.com/tunnel/features/locally-managed-tunnels/tunnel-permissions/) before choosing the account that authorizes setup. The account certificate used to create and route a locally managed tunnel can manage tunnels in that account.

Codync points the tunnel at a loopback listener that serves only the encrypted phone channel and a minimal health check. It does not publish the host's local API, terminal, OAuth callback, or webhook endpoints. Pairing and device authentication still apply. Do not put Cloudflare Access in front of the channel: its browser login flow is not part of the native app's WebSocket connection. Cloudflare supports [WebSockets](https://developers.cloudflare.com/network/websockets/); make sure WebSockets are enabled for your zone.

The tunnel provides Codync chat and control over HTTPS/WebSocket. It does not provide the private-relay TURN path used for remote screen sharing. Use Wi-Fi or Tailscale for remote screen access. If you choose **Forget this tunnel** in Codync, it removes the saved tunnel credentials and hostname from this computer; the tunnel and DNS record remain in your Cloudflare account until you delete them there.

## Your own HTTPS URL

If you already run a reverse proxy or a different tunnel, set `CODYNC_EXTERNAL_URL` on the computer to its root URL, for example `https://codync.example.com`. The proxy must forward `/channel` to the Codync host and support WebSockets. The phone adds `/channel` when it connects. Do not forward the host's local API, event stream, terminal, OAuth callback, or webhook paths to the public internet.

## Your own relay

This fork keeps its private-relay implementation unchanged. See the [private relay setup guide](https://github.com/user01816362/Codync/blob/main/private-relay/README.md) for its configuration and limits.

## If a connection fails

- Check that Codync is running on the computer and the phone is still paired with it.
- For Tailscale, check that both devices show as connected in Tailscale and belong to the same tailnet.
- For Cloudflare, keep the computer on, check **Reach from anywhere** for the tunnel status, and confirm the hostname still points to the tunnel in your Cloudflare account.
- On a local network, check that the phone and computer can reach each other and that the computer's firewall allows Codync.

A computer with no relay now advertises its private addresses on the local network, so nearby
paired phones can recover after its LAN address changes. The connection still verifies the
computer's paired key. Named Cloudflare tunnels and Tailscale remain available in this fork.
